# Accepting donations (optional)

FitLibrary is **free**. Nothing is locked behind a payment. Donations are an optional extra:

- The app never handles money. It shows a "Support the coach" link; donors pay on **your payment provider's own secure page**, so card details never touch FitLibrary and the money goes to your bank account through the provider.
- You set it up on the coach page: **Donations** tab. Leave the link empty and donations are switched off.

## Which platform

My pick is a **Yoco payment link**, with **PayFast** as the runner-up. Check the two open questions below before you commit, because I could not confirm them from public pages.

| Platform | Why / why not |
|---|---|
| **Yoco (payment link)** ← my pick | South African, pays out in rand to your SA bank account. Yoco's terms say a South African individual aged 18+ can apply (you do not need a company). Creating a profile and payment links is free; online card fees are listed at 2.55 – 2.95% excl. VAT. |
| **PayFast** (runner-up) | The most widely used SA online gateway. Offers "Pay Now" buttons and payment-request links, and publishes an instant-EFT rate of 2% (minimum R2), which is cheaper than cards for larger gifts. Its donation material is aimed at **registered charities/NPOs**, so ask whether an individual account can collect donations. |
| SnapScan / Zapper | Good for QR codes in a gym or at events. Card/wallet fees are higher (PayFast lists Zapper at 4.5% + R5). Not a good fit for an online link. |
| BackaBuddy | SA crowdfunding site, made for one-off campaigns ("help me fund X"), not an always-on tip jar. |
| Ko-fi / Buy Me a Coffee / Patreon | Popular overseas, but they pay out through PayPal or Stripe, and I could not confirm they can pay a South African bank account directly. Buy Me a Coffee also takes a 5% platform fee. I would avoid them for a "straight into my SA account" goal. |
| Plain bank transfer (EFT) | No fees, but you must publish your bank details and it cannot be a link. |

**Two things to check with Yoco or PayFast support before relying on them** (public pages did not say):
1. Can an individual / sole proprietor use a payment link to receive donations or "pay what you want" gifts? Some providers only allow sales of goods and services.
2. What are the exact fees and how long until money reaches your bank (the "settlement" time)?

## Setting it up (Yoco example)

1. Create a Yoco account and complete their identity checks (South African ID, bank account in your name).
2. In Yoco, create a **payment link**. Ask whether it can take an amount chosen by the payer; if not, create one link per amount (R20, R50, R100…).
3. In FitLibrary → coach page → **Donations**:
   - **Donation link**: the link where the donor picks any amount.
   - **Link with an amount** (optional): only if your provider lets you pass the amount in the web address. Write `{amount}` where the rand amount goes, e.g. `https://pay.example.com/you?amount={amount}`. The suggested-amount buttons (R20, R50…) then jump straight to a pre-filled payment.
   - **Message**: a sentence in your own words.
4. Tap **Test my link**, then make a small donation yourself and check it reaches your bank account.

## Money, tax and trust

- Money received from the public is normally **income**. Keep records and ask an accountant or SARS how to declare it.
- Only a registered public benefit organisation (PBO) can issue **Section 18A tax receipts**. You cannot, so do not promise them.
- Say plainly that donations are voluntary and unlock nothing. The Support page already does.
- Do not call it a "charity" or ask for money for a cause unless it is one.
